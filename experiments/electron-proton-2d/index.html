Model and what to look for
  • Atomic units (ħ = mₑ = e = 1; length a₀, energy 1 Ha = 27.2 eV, time 24.2 as). Proton fixed at the origin (it is 1836× heavier).
  • Potential V(r) = −1/√(r² + a²) with softening a = to avoid the 1/r singularity on the grid. 2D, so the level structure differs from real hydrogen.
  • Grid . Split-operator step e^{−iVΔt/2} e^{−iTΔt} e^{−iVΔt/2}, kinetic part in Fourier space. Complex absorbing potential ∝ s⁴ over the outer a₀. Reflection measured against the exact free packet: ~10⁻⁶ at k = 1, ~10⁻³ at k = 0.6, but several % for very slow waves (k ≲ 0.3, wavelength longer than the absorber).
  • Bound states are computed from the radial equation (one 1D eigenproblem per |m|) and kept only if ≥ 99 % of them lies inside the dashed circle. Very high Rydberg states do not fit on the grid and are not counted.
  • Without radiation reaction: the Hamiltonian is time-independent, so every eigenstate only picks up a phase. P(bound) stays constant (up to discretisation error) — the electron is never captured. Whatever was bound at t = 0 stays; the rest scatters and leaves through the absorber.
  • Radiation reaction (energy sink): an accelerating charge radiates (Larmor power P = 2a²/3c³) and feels a recoil force F = (2/3c³)·da/dt (Abraham–Lorentz, Landau–Lifshitz form). Here it is applied semiclassically: a is the mean Coulomb acceleration ⟨−∇V⟩, and F acts as a uniform force on the whole wavefunction. The real effect is ~10⁶–10⁸× too slow to watch (2p lifetime in hydrogen ≈ 1.6 ns ≈ 7·10⁷ a.u.), so τ = 2/3c³ = 2.6·10⁻⁷ a.u. is multiplied by the boost.
  • The Landau–Lifshitz form assumes τ ≪ the time scale of the motion. Then "work by reaction force" ≈ −"radiated". For boost ≳ 10⁶ (τ ≳ 0.3 a.u.) this no longer holds: the force still removes energy, but the numbers are qualitative. Single-pass capture of a free electron only shows up in that regime — consistent with radiative recombination being a rare process in reality.
  • Limits of the mean-field picture: a single excited eigenstate has ⟨a⟩ = 0 and never decays (real atoms do, via vacuum fluctuations); only superpositions radiate. The wavefunction stays one coherent object instead of splitting into "captured + photon" and "free" branches.
  • What to look for: slow electrons are captured much more readily than fast ones; captured population first lands in excited levels and then cascades down; a ground + 2p superposition relaxes to the ground state.
