Model and what to look for
  • Atomic units (ħ = mₑ = e = 1; length a₀, energy 1 Ha = 27.2 eV, time 24.2 as). Proton fixed at the origin (it is 1836× heavier).
  • Potential V(r) = −1/√(r² + a²) with softening a = to avoid the 1/r singularity on the grid. 2D, so the level structure differs from real hydrogen.
  • Grid . Split-operator step e^{−iVΔt/2} e^{−iTΔt} e^{−iVΔt/2}, kinetic part in Fourier space. Complex absorbing potential ∝ s⁴ over the outer a₀. Reflection measured against the exact free packet: ~10⁻⁶ at k = 1, ~10⁻³ at k = 0.6, but several % for very slow waves (k ≲ 0.3, wavelength longer than the absorber).
  • Bound states are computed from the radial equation (one 1D eigenproblem per |m|) and kept only if ≥ 99 % of them lies inside the dashed circle. Very high Rydberg states do not fit on the grid and are not counted.
  • Without radiation reaction: the Hamiltonian is time-independent, so every eigenstate only picks up a phase. P(bound) stays constant (up to discretisation error) — the electron is never captured. Whatever was bound at t = 0 stays; the rest scatters and leaves through the absorber.
  • Radiation reaction (energy sink): an accelerating charge radiates (Larmor power P = 2a²/3c³) and feels a recoil force F = (2/3c³)·da/dt (Abraham–Lorentz, Landau–Lifshitz form). Here it is applied semiclassically: a is the mean Coulomb acceleration ⟨−∇V⟩, and F acts as a uniform force on the whole wavefunction. The real effect is ~10⁶–10⁸× too slow to watch (2p lifetime in hydrogen ≈ 1.6 ns ≈ 7·10⁷ a.u.), so τ = 2/3c³ = 2.6·10⁻⁷ a.u. is multiplied by the boost.
  • The Landau–Lifshitz form assumes τ ≪ the time scale of the motion. Then "work by reaction force" ≈ −"radiated". For boost ≳ 10⁶ (τ ≳ 0.3 a.u.) this no longer holds: the force still removes energy, but the numbers are qualitative. Single-pass capture of a free electron only shows up in that regime — consistent with radiative recombination being a rare process in reality.
  • Limits of the mean-field picture: a single excited eigenstate has ⟨a⟩ = 0 and never decays (real atoms do, via vacuum fluctuations); only superpositions radiate. The wavefunction stays one coherent object instead of splitting into "captured + photon" and "free" branches.
  • Classical ensemble: N point electrons in the same potential (velocity Verlet). Initial positions are drawn from |ψ|²; momenta from a Gaussian with the local mean and spread of the Wigner function at that point (mean ∇S, covariance −¼∇∇ ln|ψ|²). This is exact for Gaussian packets and reproduces ⟨p⟩, ⟨p²⟩ and ⟨H⟩ in general, but not interference. Resampling at any time starts a fresh ensemble from the current ψ. Remaining / bound fractions are scaled by the quantum norm at the sampling time, so they compare directly with the quantum curves.
  • Classical radiation: each electron loses energy at its own Larmor rate τa² (same τ and boost), taken from its kinetic energy. Not the same force law as the quantum mean field (the Landau–Lifshitz form is unstable for point particles at large τ), but the same energy loss rate. Each classical electron radiates on its own, whereas the quantum mean field only radiates through the average dipole — and a classical bound electron keeps radiating until it sits at the bottom of the well (E = −1/a = −2 Ha), the classical "collapse of the atom". The quantum ground state does not radiate.
  • Photon field (stage 3): the electron couples to the free-space photon field: a continuum of frequencies ωⱼ (0–2 Ha, 512 modes) with polarisations x, y, at most one photon. Velocity gauge, dipole approximation: H = H_e + Σ ωⱼ a†a + Σ Gⱼ p_m (a + a†). The couplings Gⱼ² = boost·(4/3)(ωⱼ/c³)Δω/2π reproduce the real 3D spontaneous emission rate Γ = (4/3) ω |⟨f|p|i⟩|²/c³ (Fermi's golden rule), times the boost.
  • After emitting, the electron can only be in a bound level φₛ (n ≤ 3, both signs of m), so the photon part of the state is just amplitudes c(s, ω, polarisation) — the emitted photon really flies off in its mode and is never put back by hand. The coupling step is exactly unitary (it only involves the 18 vectors p_m φₛ). The coupling is ramped on over the first 15 a.u. so the electron dresses adiabatically instead of emitting a switch-on burst.
  • "Emitted" counts only photons that have left the atom (time of flight > 4 a.u.); the rest is the virtual photon cloud that every charge carries. The green density is the exact reduced electron density of the emitted part.
  • Not included: free→free emission (bremsstrahlung), a second photon (so no further cascade after a capture), the A² term.
  • Classical counterpart: each classical electron radiates at its own Larmor rate with the same boost (as in stage 2).
  • What to look for: slow electrons are captured much more readily than fast ones; captured population first lands in excited levels and then cascades down; a ground + 2p superposition relaxes to the ground state.
